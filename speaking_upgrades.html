01

Replacing ya'ni (يعني) with English Connectors

Your #1 fluency win: swapping mid-sentence Arabic fillers for a brief pause or an English linker.

When you are thinking quickly about a nuanced point, ya'ni slips in between subjects and verbs. Replacing it with a half-second silent breath—or a natural English bridge like "I mean," "actually," "so," or "say"—immediately makes your speech sound more fluent.

From your lesson

"By the way, ya'ni I am saying your name as Luis, is this ya'ni correct pronunciation?"

Natural Upgrade

"By the way, I'm pronouncing your name as Luis—is that the right pronunciation?"

From your lesson

"To be honest, before ya'ni I check the facts..."

Natural Upgrade

"To be honest, before I looked into the facts..."

From your lesson

"But ya'ni honestly after I ya'ni checked more about this topic, I ya'ni understood more details..."

Natural Upgrade

"Honestly, though, once I read more about this topic, I understood a lot more of the details..."

From your lesson

"If they have ya'ni a certain justification..." / "Yes, ya'ni I am talking as a third party."

Natural Upgrade

"If they had, say, a certain justification..." / "Yes—I mean, I'm speaking as an outside observer."

Quick Replacement Menu: Use "I mean," to clarify • "so," to move forward • "say," to give an example • a silent pause to buy thinking time.
02

Irregular Verbs, Passives & Past Questions

Small grammar slips that are easy to eliminate once you spot the pattern.

Irregular Verb: cut

"already cutted the supplies"

✓ "had already cut the supplies"

Rule: cut – cut – cut never takes -ed (just like put, set, cost).

Noun vs. Verb after "from"

"already suffered from destroy and suffered from everything"

✓ "had already suffered widespread destruction"

Rule: After suffer from, use a noun (destruction, devastation), not the verb destroy.

Plural Passive Agreement

"thousand of people was killed in the beginning"

✓ "thousands of people were killed initially"

Rule: Without a specific number in front, use plural thousands of people + were.

Past-Tense Question Auxiliary

"so why they send and drop the other one?"

✓ "so why did they send and drop the second one?"

Rule: Past questions need did + subject + base verb.

Past-Tense Consistency in Storytelling

"make the life of the Japanese people... horrible, bombed the..." → "made life miserable for the Japanese people and bombed the..."

03

High-Impact Word Precision

Look-alike pairs and collocations that carry big meaning in historical and analytical discussions.

What Was Said Target Word / Phrase Why It Matters
"the causality or coshalities" casualties /ˈkæʒuəltiz/ Casualties = people killed or injured. Causality = cause-and-effect relationship.
"should surround themselves to" surrender to Surrender = give up / capitulate. Surround = encircle.
"stricted monitoring on what to be disclosed" strict monitoring of what could be disclosed Strict is already an adjective (no -ed), and monitoring pairs naturally with of.
"no one declared a real figure" no one released / published real figures We declare war or independence, but we release / publish / disclose figures or data.
"become more focus and concentrating" became more focused and concentrated Use the adjective focused (or say "shifted their focus to other priorities").
"till the last solider" to the last soldier /ˈsəʊldʒər/ Watch the -dier sound (/dʒ/) so it stays distinct from solid.
04

Adverb Placement, Time & Natural Stance Phrasing

Smoothing out word order so your sentences flow effortlessly.

Time Adverb Position

"I hope you had today a safe trip back from China."

Natural English Order

"I hope you had a safe trip back from China today."

Keep the verb (had) and its object (a safe trip) together; put today at the end.

Telling the Time

"I am still in the office, it is now 6 and 24."

Natural English

"I'm still in the office—it's 6:24 now."

Say "six twenty-four" without and.

Polite Preference Question

"What is the most favorable pronunciation your ear would like to hear?"

Concise & Natural

"Which pronunciation do you prefer?" / "Which version sounds best to you?"

Expressing a Shifted View

"So now I become more with the opinion that..."

C1/C2 Upgrade

"So now I lean more toward the view that..." / "I've come to agree more with the idea that..."
